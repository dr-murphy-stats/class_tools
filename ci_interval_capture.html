What do you notice?

  1. Did your interval from Part 1 capture p? Could you have known that without seeing the solid line?
  2. Look at the intervals that miss. Where are their sample proportions p compared with p?
  3. After 100 or more intervals, how close is the percent captured to the confidence level? Why isn't it exactly equal?
  4. Switch from 95% to 90%, then to 99%. What happens to the widths of the intervals and to the percent captured?
  5. Try left-handed students with n = 30. What happens to the percent captured, and which intervals are dashed?
  6. In Part 4, how does the margin of error change from 90% to 99% confidence? Which number in the formula ME = zc · sp is changing?
  7. In Part 5, about how much larger must n be to cut the margin of error in half? Why does the square root in sp explain this?
  8. Is there a trade-off between confidence and precision? How could you get both a high confidence level and a narrow interval?
